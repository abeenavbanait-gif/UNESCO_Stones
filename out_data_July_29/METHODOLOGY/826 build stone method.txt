### 1. The NLP Methodology & Scanning Process
I built a multi-layered NLP (Natural Language Processing) script in Python (`classify_monuments_v2.py`). It scans the **Site Name**, **Brief Description**, and the full **OUV (Outstanding Universal Value) Statement** for every single site. 
- **Lemmatization:** Before scanning, the AI uses `spaCy` to "lemmatize" the text. This strips words down to their grammatical roots (e.g., turning *"quarries"* into *"quarry"*, or *"carved"* into *"carve"*) so that pluralizations or verb tenses don't trick the scanner.
- **Mega-Dictionary Pattern Matching:** I compiled 8 massive dictionaries containing hundreds of specific geological and architectural terms. The script scans the lemmatized text against these dictionaries:
  - **Igneous Rocks:** *granite, basalt, diorite, porphyry, obsidian, tuff...*
  - **Sedimentary Rocks:** *sandstone, limestone, travertine, shale, breccia...*
  - **Metamorphic Rocks:** *marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss, schist...*
  - **Named / Trade Stones:** *carrara marble, caen stone, pentelic marble, portland stone...*
  - **Construction Terms:** *ashlar, masonry, monolith, megalith, rubble, opus incertum...*
- **The Scoring System:** 
  - If a site's name contains a structural keyword (like "Cathedral", "Fortress", "Aqueduct", "Cave"), it gets a massive score boost.
  - If the OUV statement explicitly mentions geological stones (e.g. "built of local limestone"), the score goes up based on how many unique stones are found.
  - If the site hits "Exclusion" keywords (e.g. "Cultural Landscape", "Fossil", "Rock Art"), the score is heavily penalized so we don't accidentally classify a natural canyon or a cave painting as a built monument.

### 2. The Final Filtering (How we got 826)

1. It achieved a `HIGH` or `MEDIUM` confidence score from the NLP density check.

3. In borderline cases where the NLP was unsure, it passed the text to the **Gemini 3.5 Flash LLM** for a semantic read, and the AI confirmed the presence of geological building materials.
